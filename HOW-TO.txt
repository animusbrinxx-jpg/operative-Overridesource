DEDSEC FIELD GUIDE // OPERATIVE OVERRIDE

You’ve got access to the personnel file. Try not to turn anyone into a polygon.

GET CONNECTED
Launch Watch Dogs: Legion and load into your single-player save first. Open Operative Override, then click Connect / Refresh team. This version supports the verified Steam DirectX 11 build; it will stop if your game version is unsupported.

PICK YOUR TARGET
Select the operative you want to edit. The safest approach is to play as someone else while you edit them. Switch away in-game, then tick the inactive-operative confirmation. That checkbox is your confirmation, not an automatic check.

REWRITE THE LOOK
For major body or gender changes, start with a compatible team member: choose them under Copy team look, then click Use appearance. Customize that look afterward. A gender selection alone does not choose a matching body. Follow the editor’s compatibility warnings; unusual combinations may not look right.

On Body & voice, Apply appearance & voice applies your staged look and general voice. On Outfit, Apply clothing only applies clothing without intentionally changing body, gender, voice, grooming, tattoos or gameplay traits. Check clothing in-game: not every catalog piece fits every body.

Wardrobe presets save the outfit currently worn in-game, not unapplied choices in the editor. Give the outfit a name, then select it later and use Apply saved outfit only. Presets are restricted to the same operative and body model. Some outfits cannot safely be applied by this version; the editor explains why.

Supported appearance, gender, general voice, grooming, tattoos and clothing edits have persisted through saving, quitting, relaunching and changing locations in testing. Change one major thing at a time when experimenting. Switching a look does not give you the source operative’s perks or equipment.

VOICE WARNING
Special and premium operatives may have Ubisoft-recorded audio separate from their general voice. Wrench is confirmed: his original grunts, vehicle-stealing reactions, unique ability callouts and other bespoke audio can remain his original voice. Changing his general voice can therefore produce mixed voices.

Keep Wrench’s original voice for the most consistent experience. You can still choose another voice. Other special operatives may behave similarly, but this has not been confirmed for every one of them.

GET THEM BACK
Undo reverses the most recent recorded edit. It stops if later changes would be overwritten. Switch to someone else first.

Restore Baseline returns appearance and recorded voice settings to the earliest safe state Operative Override recorded for that operative. It works across multiple edits. That state is not guaranteed to be the character’s factory-original look. If no suitable recorded state exists, the editor stops. A missing recorded NPC voice setting is left unchanged and explained before restoration.

Supported edits create recovery backups automatically. Restoration creates a fresh backup too, so you can Undo the restoration. Keep the app’s Backups folder with the app: it holds your recovery history. Save your game after you have checked the result.

IF LONDON GETS WEIRD
Switch away from the edited operative and back again. If portraits or other visuals still look wrong, save and reload the game. Returning to the main menu was enough to refresh portraits in our tests; you may need to relaunch if they remain stale.

Check both the playable operative and their street appearance after a major change. If the result still looks wrong, Undo or Restore Baseline rather than piling on more changes.

Weapons and custom in-game bio text are not v1 features.
